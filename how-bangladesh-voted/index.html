How Bangladesh Voted

The result looked simple: 209 seats for the BNP. But inside 42,382 polling centres, Bangladesh’s landslide broke into thousands of sharply different local contests.

Nearly 180 million people live in Bangladesh. The country had waited since 2008 for a genuinely competitive parliamentary election. We followed the vote from the national map into city wards, small towns and villages — and found an election that changed depending on how closely you looked.

The cities did not vote like the rest of Bangladesh

A generation with no election to look back on

Some 44 million people on the roll were too young to vote in 2008. They were not necessarily first-time voters. But 2026 was the first genuinely competitive parliamentary election of their adult lives. Together, they made up more than a third of the decoded roll.

One building, two electorates

Across Bangladesh, one school or community hall can contain two polling centres: one for women, another for men. Those paired rooms offer an unusually close look at the two electorates. What they show is not one national “women’s vote.” Place changes the picture.

Communities

What the seat map hid about minority communities

No ballot records a voter’s religion, and neither does the voter roll. We used names on the roll to estimate where minority communities were concentrated. This is not a count of Hindu, Buddhist, Christian or Indigenous votes. It is a map of where those communities were registered.

Local borders The parliamentary map breaks into 4,539 unions. Jamaat led in — of them; the BNP in —. In 751 unions, the leader differed from the parent upazila. An average union has about 22,000 registered voters. A parliamentary seat has roughly 420,000.
Towns sometimes broke from the countryside around them. Forty-five of 319 municipalities had a different leader from their parent upazila; 35 were direct BNP–Jamaat reversals. In 213 towns, participation was lower than in the surrounding unions.
Cities splintered ward by ward. In 102 of 463 wards, the leading party was not the party that led the city as a whole. City wards also produced the largest share of margins below five points.

The national vote, redrawn along local borders

The same parliamentary returns draw a different map at union, town, ward and upazila level. It is a starting point for the coming local contests, not a forecast. Local candidates, alliances and turnout will change the race.

What the national map cannot show

The verdict was decisive. The country beneath it was not.

The BNP won 209 of the 297 declared seats. That is the result of the election. It is not a portrait of the electorate.

Open those seats and a more complicated country appears. Participation fell sharply in the cities. Centres inside the same constituency often produced strikingly different results. Women-only centres followed different patterns in villages, towns and cities. And a divide involving minority-heavy unions emerged only after the seats were broken apart.

The returns cannot tell us the age, sex or faith behind a ballot. But they can show where the result changes when the border changes. A national tally tells us who won power. The smaller returns show the country that produced it.

Methodology, sources and limitations

Published sources

The national seat tally follows the Election Commission gazette, as reported by Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha. The Election Commission result portal is the source for the released returns. The EC’s party-wise vote release and the coverage of the centre-level file are documented by The Daily Star. The description of 2026 as the first genuinely competitive parliamentary election since 2008 follows the EU Election Observation Mission. The population description uses the United Nations’ World Population Prospects 2024.

Scope

The two datasets

Results. The Election Commission's centre-level count: 42,382 polling centres across 297 constituencies. Polling in Sherpur-3 was postponed; Chattogram-2 and Chattogram-4 are absent from this centre file. Vote shares use valid ballots. The story labels valid ballots ÷ registered voters as the valid-ballot rate (58.9%); including rejected ballots as participation produces 60.2%. National winner counts use the official gazette. Five winner-party labels are corrected in the headline tally and constituency map; derived subgroup files retain their original classification unless a correction is stated. The EC’s later party-wise release reported 49.97% for the BNP and 31.76% for Jamaat; the centre file used consistently for subgroup calculations produces 50.15% and 31.54%.

The roll. The 2026 roll corpus for 292 constituencies — 199,846 PDFs and 125,104,801 records — was extracted and decoded by this desk, including reconstruction of the nonstandard embedded font encoding in the source files (99.996% of rows fully clean). Outputs are published only as aggregates at union level or coarser; no row-level derivative is released.

Joining the two

Age

The roll also contains — registrations marked hijra. That figure is reported as an administrative roll category; the analysis does not infer gender from names.

Religion

The name-token classifier was developed by hand-labelling surname cases, with given, parent and spouse-name tokens used to resolve some ambiguous surnames. It operates on 125,104,801 raw roll records, including duplicate registrations; 90,154 records (0.07%) were left unresolved. It estimates the roll, not identity or voting behaviour. “Indigenous” is an ethnicity category, not a religion, and is therefore included only in the broader “minority-community” measure. The upazila religion layer has a much higher unresolved rate and is not used for claims in this version.

Single-sex-centre comparisons and rejected ballots

Sex-specific figures come from the 10,468 single-sex centres in the 226 constituencies where they hold at least 10% of the electorate — the inclusion rule used throughout this story. Rejected-ballot rates are invalid ballots over cast ballots (valid + rejected) at those centres; the sex gap carries a 95% interval from resampling whole seats 1,500 times and is significant overall and within each settlement type separately. The same-building analysis matches exact normalized centre names within a constituency, retaining boys’ and girls’ branch qualifiers. It contains 1,204 buildings and 2,813 centres. Estimates are mean per-building differences; intervals resample whole constituencies. The pooled match is not reported because 41% of matched buildings are in city corporations, against 31% of scoped centres. Neither the aggregate nor matched comparison is an individual ballot record.

Centre and union level

Centre-level figures use all 42,382 published results. The party histograms and variance decompositions include only seats each party contested — Jamaat fielded no candidate in 72 of 297 seats, the BNP in 9 — because a centre in an uncontested seat records a structural zero, not weak support. Including those zeroes would put Jamaat's "between-constituency" variance share at 81% instead of 50% and make its support look far more regional than it is. The decomposition weights each centre by valid ballots and compares the variance of constituency means with the total.

Union-level figures come from re-aggregating the whole roll by the district, upazila and union recorded inside each source PDF, then matching those Bengali names to the election file's English ones within each already-validated upazila — a search space of about ten candidates, scored on transliteration similarity and electorate size, with anything ambiguous left unmatched. 4,325 of 4,539 unions matched (95.3%), size correlation 0.954, median roll-to-register ratio 1.01. After dropping units where the two disagree by more than a factor of two, 4,281 unions across 267 constituencies remain; 4,280 have a matched geometry in the union map. Religion at this resolution uses the project's own classifier — the tie-break ladder over each voter's name, father's, mother's and husband's — leaving 0.07% unresolved, the same rate as the seat layer. City-corporation wards are excluded: a city corporation spans many thanas, so the roll's blocks do not map onto it one-to-one, and the attempted match produced a size correlation of 0.23, i.e. wrong pairings. Municipalities were not attempted.

The within-constituency estimator. Each union is compared with the electorate-weighted mean of all matched unions in its own constituency, including itself. This holds constituency-level candidate and regional context constant; local campaigning and other union characteristics can still differ. Only seats with at least four matched unions are used. Confidence intervals resample whole constituencies 1,000 times. This is what makes the scale comparison possible: between-seat and within-seat estimates of the same quantity can disagree in size and, for age and turnout, in sign.

Ecological inference — the standing caveat

Every age and community-composition comparison is ecological. A place with a younger or more minority-classified roll giving a party more support does not establish that those individuals chose it. Within-seat comparisons remove constituency-level context, but remain comparisons among unions. Single-sex centres directly observe ballots cast at sex-restricted sites, yet they still aggregate many people and can differ by location.

The local aggregation

2026 national votes aggregated inside each computable local boundary: 4,539 union parishads, 491 upazila parishads, 319 pourashavas, 463 city-corporation wards and 12 city corporations — 5,824 overlapping administrative units. Another 54 union shapes have no result or no centre. Khelafat candidates are counted as a separate category, matching the unit map. Tiers are displayed as alternative geographies and are not independent electorates. The local-margin chart uses the recorded lead between the first- and second-place candidates inside each computable unit.

Uncertainty

Seat-level correlations are Pearson, weighted by registered voters, over the — seats that survive the join guard. Intervals are cluster bootstraps resampling whole constituencies — centres within a seat are not independent. Results whose intervals cross zero are described as not distinguishable from zero, and are drawn grey.

Privacy

The roll is public but personal. The smallest published roll-derived geography here is a matched rural union, averaging about 22,000 registered voters. No individual-level derivative, name or row is published.
